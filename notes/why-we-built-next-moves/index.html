Why we built Next Moves

Sumit Jaswal, Founder, Provenexa Research · 26 November 2026

If you sell to companies, you know this line: "We already have a vendor."

You hear it on a call you made in good faith. The company really did need what you sell. You just called a few months too late.

In over two decades of sales, I heard that line more times than I can count. It is rarely about price, and rarely about your pitch. It is about timing. When a company is setting up, someone picks the vendors early and quietly. Most sellers find out about the company only after that.

Companies move in steps

A new plant or office does not appear overnight. It moves in steps. First a new company or a plot of land. Then an approval. Then a few first hires. Then the vendors are picked. Then it opens, and everyone arrives at once.

SeptemberZF Steering Gear's unit bought more land next to its project at Supa, in Maharashtra, for its aluminium plant.

NovemberIts aluminium division was reported to have started commercial production there.

Both were public steps, with dates. Between them were about ten weeks when a seller could have called at the right moment.

Most people never see these steps. They are spread across many places and many languages, and nobody joins them up for one company. So the news arrives late, all at once, and often mixed with old stories passed off as new.

A note I used to write by hand

At Monster, in 2011, every account manager kept a note for each client. It said who they are, what is changing there, and who to call. We wrote it by hand, and it went stale the week we wrote it. Next Moves is that note, kept current for you.

What Next Moves does

Next Moves follows those early steps for thousands of Indian companies, every day. For each company it shows:

  • What moved, with the date and the kind of source behind it.
  • Why now: where the company is on its way from first step to opening.
  • Who to approach: the role that usually picks vendors at that stage.

You check it before you call. Then you use the contact tool you already have to reach the person. We come before that tool, not instead of it.

The rules we keep

Every step has a date and a source. If only a newspaper says it, we mark it "reported". If the company said it, we say so. If we do not know something, we say "not known yet" instead of guessing. A wrong date or a mixed-up company costs you a call, so we check before we show.
